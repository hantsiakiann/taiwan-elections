How the forecast works

Fundamentals. For every county and special municipality, the model measures partisan lean from the most recent presidential and party-list legislative votes (the two are nearly identical across counties, r = 0.997, so they're averaged), the previous mayoral result relative to that year's national environment, and whether an incumbent is on the ballot. These are fit to the 2009/10, 2014, 2018 and 2022 mayoral elections. In leave-one-cycle-out backtests the fundamentals alone miss a race's two-camp share by about on average, beating a presidential-lean-only baseline in every cycle.

National environment. All four past cycles show the governing party losing ground in local elections relative to the preceding presidential vote (about 10–12 points of two-camp share each time). The prior for 2026 is centered on the DPP's 46% average from the 2018 and 2022 midterms, with a wide spread so polls can move it.

Polls. Each poll is converted to the DPP share among voters choosing between the two main camps. Its error combines sampling error (with a 1.5 design effect, counting only decided respondents), non-sampling error, a drift term that grows with the poll's age, a polling miss shared by every poll in that race, and one shared by every poll nationwide. Polls sponsored by DPP-aligned or KMT-aligned groups get house effects that are estimated from races where they overlap with media and academic polls. Polls are the final-matchup tables on the zh.wikipedia polling page, limited to the registered candidates; hypothetical pairings and AI-simulated "polls" are excluded. Opt-in online panels get extra error.

Combining them. Every piece is Gaussian on the log-odds scale, so the posterior is computed exactly. Races with many recent polls follow the polls; races with one old poll stay close to fundamentals; and the weight on polls rises as election day nears. Each of the 10,000 simulations draws the national environment, house effects and race results jointly, then adds drift for the weeks remaining. Polled third candidates take a share off the top, and the winner is whoever has the most votes.

Limits. Miaoli, Nantou, Chiayi County, Pingtung, Taitung and Lienchiang have no published final-matchup polls and run on fundamentals alone; several other races have one or two. Wikipedia does not list sample sizes, so 1,000 is assumed. Kinmen and Lienchiang have no DPP contest and use a simple KMT-vs-strongest-independent prior (Kinmen's informed by its one poll). Third-candidate shares are drawn independently of the two-camp split.
